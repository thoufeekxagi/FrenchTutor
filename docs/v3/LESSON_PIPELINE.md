# V3 lesson pipeline: how units are written, checked and turned into the app

This is the playbook for creating A2, B1 and B2 in the same way and at the same
quality as the authored A1 course. It records the plan, the method, the style,
the sources, and the mistakes the checkers already caught.

Short version: **every unit is written by hand, once, as plain Python data in a
chapter file, then exported into the app lesson files (the product).** A PDF is
an optional reference artifact after a level's source content is ready; it is
not a prerequisite for authoring, exporting or using app lessons. Nothing is
generated by an AI at runtime and nothing in `flutter_app/assets/content/tcf/`
is edited by hand.

## 1. Why this way

- The owner is learning French from this app, and wants other people to learn
  from it. Quality must not drop. A deterministic, reviewed course beats
  generated lessons: same order, same words, same answers for everyone.
- A single master text means the PDF, the app, the stories and the tests can
  never disagree.
- The app is the product. The PDF exists so a human (and the next agent) can
  read the whole course quickly and check it. Do not polish the PDF at the
  expense of the app.
- The learner's path, always in this order: sounds and spelling, words,
  phrases and sentence building, grammar (how words connect), dialogues and
  stories, then listening, speaking, reading and writing, then TCF-style
  practice.

## 3. The unit recipe

Every A1 unit has the same ten sections, in this order (see
`chapters/14_unit14.py` as the model). A2 keeps the same complete skill path,
with vocabulary and task targets from `syllabus/a2.json`; each authored unit
becomes lessons in the app.

| Section | Contents | App lessons |
|---|---|---|
| N.1 Words | A1 uses 5 groups. A2 uses the 5 or 6 authored groups and per-unit word target in its syllabus/source. Every entry has an English sound guide, meaning and example sentence (fr + en). | one word lesson per authored group, with generated "what does it mean" and "which word did you hear" checks |
| N.2 Phrases | useful chunks used whole | phrase lesson (say aloud) |
| N.3 Grammar | one idea per card, plain English first, tables, "watch out" and "in Canada" notes | explain and table steps |
| N.4 Dialogues | two short dialogues using only taught words, with `tu` or `vous` stated | explain and speaker steps |
| N.5 Story | 6 to 8 short sentences, Canadian setting | a story in the reader, plus its quiz |
| N.6 Listening | 5 single short sentences, 4 options | listen-once questions |
| N.7 Reading | 5 signs or short notices, 4 options | choose steps |
| N.8 Writing | a guided task with a model answer and a checklist | explain/write step |
| N.9 Speaking | a short directed task with a model and tips | speak step |
| N.10 TCF corner and checkpoint | 4 listening and 3 reading in exam format, then a 10-question checkpoint | TCF lesson, checkpoint lesson |

Stage 0 (sounds) has its own shape: 13 lessons from "How French works" to a
first Canadian listening. It is the model for pronunciation content.

B1 and B2 use the same deterministic source-to-app pipeline with a
level-specific lesson shape: five six-term vocabulary sets, reusable phrases,
grammar, dialogues, listening, reading, writing, speaking, TCF practice and a
checkpoint. B1 Unit 1 and B2 Unit 1 each add a separate four-skill readiness
lesson. B1 source is `book/b1_source.py` with its eighteen-row-per-unit
vocabulary extension and adapters `chapters/27_b1_unit01.py` through
`38_b1_unit12.py`. B2 source is `book/b2_source.py` with its vocabulary
extension and adapters `chapters/39_b2_unit01.py` through
`chapters/52_b2_unit14.py`. Each level teaches 30 focus terms per unit and 8
reusable chunks. Run `validate_b1.py` or `validate_b2.py` after exporting. The
app lists the levels in A1 → A2 → B1 → B2 order, but does not enforce a CEFR
prerequisite gate.

## 4. Writing method for one unit (follow in order)

1. Read the unit's row in `docs/v3/ROADMAP.md` and its syllabus entry. Note the
   grammar code (for example g15), the Canadian hook, the situations.
2. Read the previous unit's chapter file for tone, and the word list so far
   (`python3 content/tcf/book/check_book.py --verbose` lists notes).
3. Write the new chapter source. For A1/A2, use `chapters/NN_unitNN.py` or the
   A2 unit adapter. For B1 and B2, edit the corresponding unit in
   `book/b1_source.py` or `book/b2_source.py` and retain its numbered adapter.
   Write every word, sentence, table, dialogue, story and question yourself.
   Do not paste from another source.
4. Run `python3 content/tcf/book/check_book.py`. Fix until 0 errors and 0
   ledger warnings.
5. Add the unit's story to `story_gloss.py`: summary, key words, and a meaning
   for every token the word tables do not already cover.
6. Export only the stage being changed: `python3 content/tcf/book/export_app.py
   --a2-only`, `--b1-only` or `--b2-only`. A full export rewrites every
   generated lesson and story. The exporter prints any story word without a
   meaning; fix and rerun the scoped export.
7. Run `python3 content/tcf/tools/validate.py` (0 errors expected).
8. **Blind audit:** re-solve every listening, reading and checkpoint question
   from the text alone, without looking at the key. Compare. Any mismatch is a
   bug in the key or the question.
9. Check arithmetic and facts in the unit (see section 6).
10. `flutter test --no-pub test/v3_lesson_test.dart test/v3_story_test.dart test/v3_layout_test.dart`.
11. Do not build a PDF as a gate for app lessons. Finish and validate the level's source units first; make a reference PDF after the level is complete or when the owner asks. Do not commit or push unless asked.
12. Update `docs/v3/ROADMAP.md` (status) and `content/tcf/REVIEW_NEEDED.md`
    (new points a fluent speaker should confirm).
13. On a device, play the new lessons with the course walk (section 8 of
    `FAST_ITERATION_TESTING.md` for the manual listening part).

## 5. Style card (what "our quality" means)

Language and level

- True beginner first. Plain English explanation before any French rule. One
  idea per card. Short sentences. No jargon without a one-line gloss.
- Cumulative vocabulary: a word may appear in a dialogue, story or question
  only after it was taught (or in the tiny always-known set). The checker
  enforces it. Names and places need adding to `PROPER` in `check_book.py`.
- Canadian context in every unit: real situations (health card, SIN, lease,
  clinic, bus pass, winter). Mark Canada versus France differences only where
  they are verified (déjeuner/dîner/souper, courriel, magasiner, fin de
  semaine, 24-hour clock on schedules). Teach clear standard French for
  production; Québec colour goes in `CANADA(...)` notes.
- Tone: adult, calm, respectful. No childish praise, no gamification language.

Words and sound guides

- Each row: French, English sound guide, English meaning, one example sentence
  with its English line. **Every word the learner studies has an example
  sentence. `check_book.py` fails otherwise.** Phrase lists (sections titled
  "Phrases") are sentences already.
- Sound guides are plain English approximations with stress in capitals and
  `(n)` for a nasal vowel, for example `boh(n)-ZHOOR` for bonjour. IPA only in
  a detail. Guides are approximations; a human must confirm them.
- Examples: short (about 3 to 7 words), natural, concrete, using taught words.
  Prefer a sentence that shows the word's real use over a definition.
- Numbers and money must add up. Re-check every price, tax and total by hand.

Grammar

- Plain English first, then a small table, then examples, then a "watch out".
- Show the spelling or sound change that trips learners (for example
  acheter/payer spelling change, fils with the s said).

Dialogues and stories

- Two or three speakers at most. Recurring characters are fine (Sam, Marc,
  Nadia, Léa, Priya, Karim, Samira, Awa, Diallo, Haddad, Tremblay). Keep facts
  consistent across units (a past mistake: two characters were "neighbours" but
  lived in different cities; now they are classmates in Ottawa).
- A story is 6 to 8 sentences, one Canadian scene, only taught words, with
  an English line for every sentence. Each story needs three quiz questions
  with four options.

Questions (TCF style)

- Listening: one short sentence, played once, 4 options, one correct, plausible
  distractors built on real beginner errors (numbers, times, prices, similar
  sounds). Reading: signs, notices, short messages.
- Never two identical options. Never a correct answer that depends on a word not
  yet taught. Explanations are one short sentence.
- Wording of the exam is original. Official material sets format and targets,
  never text.

## 6. Mistakes the checkers and audits already caught (learn from them)

| Mistake | Where | Guard |
|---|---|---|
| Duplicate answer options | Unit 4, 4.10C q2 | `check_book.py` rejects duplicates |
| Words used before taught (including a grammar form) | Units 2 and 3 | vocabulary ledger |
| Café arithmetic that did not add up | Unit 5 | hand check; now 6.00 + GST 0.30 + QST 0.60 = 6.90 (QST 9.975 percent of 6.00 is about 0.60) |
| Missing English line for an example | export | exporter error |
| A French line over the length limit | export | exporter error |
| Story keyword written `autobus` while the story word is `l'autobus` | story gloss | export check: keyword must be a story word |
| Story word with no meaning | story | exporter lists it |
| 156 of 290 words with no example sentence | Units 1 to 5 and Stage 0 | 178 sentences written; checker now blocks a word without one |
| Character facts inconsistent | Unit 1 dialogue | read dialogues for continuity |

## 7. The PDF

- Purpose: the owner studies from it and a human reviews it. It is made from the
  same chapter files, so it is always in step with the app.
- Build: `python3 content/tcf/book/build_book.py`. It needs Google Chrome at
  `/Applications/Google Chrome.app` (headless print to PDF).
- Output: `docs/v3/book/ParleSprint_A1_Book.pdf` and `.html`. Committed.
- The complete A2 reviewer copy is built from all twelve A2 chapters with
  `python3 content/tcf/book/build_a2_review.py`. It writes
  `docs/v3/book/ParleSprint_A2_Reviewer_Draft.pdf` and `.html`; inspect the
  result and leave human French/TCF review open until a specialist signs off.
- Copy to the owner's Desktop when asked:
  `cp docs/v3/book/ParleSprint_A1_Book.pdf ~/Desktop/ParleSprint_A1_Book_<range>.pdf`.
- For A1, the builder writes only the A1 reference book. Build it when the
  owner requests it or after a planned A1 content pass; do not block app lesson
  authoring on PDF production. A2/B1/B2 reference books should be built only
  after their source units are complete and the owner wants those artifacts.
- Still to add to the book: A1 grammar summary on two pages, numbers and dates
  reference, a Canadian French mini-dictionary, a 14 to 18 week study calendar,
  a printable progress sheet (listed in `ROADMAP.md`).

## 8. Exporting to the app

`python3 content/tcf/book/export_app.py` rewrites all generated lesson and story
files, `course.json` and `stories.json`. Use `--a2-only`, `--b1-only` or
`--b2-only` to replace one authored stage and preserve the other stages. A full
export regenerates every authored stage from source. The A2-only check/export
also skips B1/B2 source imports, so unfinished later-stage authoring cannot
block an A2 refinement. Current output is listed in `ROADMAP.md`.
What it generates itself is limited to "what does this word mean" and "which
word did you hear" checks built from the authored list. It invents no French.
Exercises become multiple choice only when four distinct short options exist;
otherwise they stay on paper. Details: `CONTENT_AUTHORING.md`, `LESSON_FORMAT.md`,
`STORY_FORMAT.md`.

How a lesson is spoken: the speaker button asks the live tutor to say the exact
line (see `MARIE_TUTOR.md`). There is no audio catalog to bake for lessons.

## 9. Sources and research (what was used, and how)

Use public sources for targets and patterns only. Never copy text.

| Need | Source | How it was or should be used |
|---|---|---|
| TCF Canada format, timing, scales | France Éducation international (FEI) candidate documents; public TCF preparation pages | structure and question style. Public pages describe A1 items as short everyday documents with four options, listening played once in exam conditions, difficulty rising from A1 to C2. **Verify numbers on the FEI site before release** (listening and reading 39 questions each, writing 3 tasks in 60 minutes, speaking 3 tasks in 12 minutes) |
| Immigration targets | IRCC and CCLB NCLC/CLB descriptors | can-do goals per unit; NCLC 7 is the common target. Never promise a score |
| Level content | CEFR inventories (Council of Europe, EAQUALS, Beacco référentiels) | grammar and function lists as a spine |
| Word frequency | Lexique 383 (CC BY-SA 4.0, attribute) | choosing and ranking words |
| Example patterns | Tatoeba (CC BY 2.0 FR, attribute) | patterns only, rewrite every sentence |
| Canadian terms | TERMIUM Plus (Government of Canada open data; verify licence before shipping) | terminology reference |
| Québec usage | OQLF Banque de dépannage and GDT | reference only (CC BY-NC-SA, never copy into the app) |
| Candidate word pool | `flutter_app/assets/content/vocab_phase1..3.json` (1,392 legacy words: survival, everyday, CLB 7) | a pool to pick from, never dumped into A1; every sound guide and example is written fresh |
| UI references | Speak, Babbel, Memrise, Vocabulary.com screenshots (local `references/`, not in git) | structure only; colours, fonts and buttons stay ours |

## 11. Definition of done for a unit

- `check_book.py`: 0 errors, 0 ledger warnings.
- `export_app.py`: no story word without a meaning, 0 errors.
- `validate.py`: 0 errors.
- Blind audit finished, zero mismatches.
- Arithmetic and Canadian facts checked.
- Flutter content tests green when the owner requests them. PDF production is
  deferred until a level's authored source is complete or the owner requests it.
- `ROADMAP.md` and `REVIEW_NEEDED.md` updated.
