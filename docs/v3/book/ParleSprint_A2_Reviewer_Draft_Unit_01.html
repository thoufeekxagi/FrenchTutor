Learning design and pacing

The learner is an adult beginner in Canada who often studies with ParleSprint on an iPad beside a notes app. Each unit uses a short scene, concise English support, French listening and reading, active notes, closed-note recall, speaking or writing, and a changed-context transfer attempt. A proposed review schedule begins at days 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30; the next review should move sooner when recall fails.

The A2 plan allocates 14 to 16 focused hours per unit (168 to 192 hours across twelve units). This is a planning range, not a promise or a calendar deadline. Learners move on when delayed recall and unfamiliar-context use are reliable.

The design uses spaced retrieval because second-language research supports delayed practice on average, while individual results vary. AI tutor practice is a way to create guided attempts and feedback, not a claim that this app makes every learner faster. Research references: Kim and Webb (2022), DOI 10.1111/lang.12479; Li, Wang and Yang (2025), DOI 10.1111/jcal.70060.

1.3 Grammar: connect ideas and explain why (g23)

At A1, you learned to make clear short sentences. At A2, keep the same clear word order and connect two ideas so the listener can follow your schedule, reason or choice. A connector joins ideas; it does not change the verb form.

A. add, contrast and give a reason

Choose the relationship between the ideas
ConnectorWhat it doesExample
etadds informationJe travaille le matin et j'étudie le soir.
maisshows a contrastJe suis occupé lundi, mais je suis libre mardi.
parce quegives a reasonJe reporte le cours parce que je travaille.
doncshows a resultLe bus est en retard, donc j'arrive plus tard.
  • Say the two ideas first. Then choose the connector that shows their relationship: addition, contrast, reason or result.
  • After parce que, use a subject and a verb: parce que je travaille.
  • Use donc for the result: Je travaille, donc je reporte le cours.
  • Avoid joining every sentence with et. A precise connector helps another person understand your point.

B. put events in an easy order

A small sequence makes a longer answer easier to follow
OrderExample
firstD'abord, je vérifie mon horaire.
nextEnsuite, je planifie mes tâches.
lastEnfin, je prépare mon sac.

1.9 Speaking: explain your week, then adapt

SPEAKING 1.9S
Answer follow-up questions
First answer without notes for 30-45 seconds: what does a usual week look like, what is one priority, and when do you study or rest? Add one reason or contrast. Then ask Marie for one follow-up question. If needed, request one hint, try again, and ask Marie to change one day or responsibility so you must adapt the answer.
  • Answer the question asked; do not recite the model.
  • Use one connector to explain a reason and one to show a contrast or result.
  • If you do not understand, ask for repetition or clarification in French.
  • After one correction, repeat the answer with a changed day or activity.

Reading 1.10C: Checkpoint: 10 questions
1. A A habit. Une habitude means a habit.
2. B en général. En général means generally.
3. C A deadline. Une échéance is a deadline.
4. C parce que. Parce que introduces the reason.
5. A donc. Donc introduces the result.
6. B To rest. Se reposer means to rest.
7. A An unexpected event. Un imprévu is an unexpected event.
8. B To clarify or specify. Préciser means to specify or clarify.
9. C D'abord, ensuite, enfin. Use d'abord, then ensuite, and finally enfin.
10. A Je reporte le cours parce que j'ai un imprévu au travail.. Parce que introduces the reason: an unexpected work problem. The other sentences either reverse the cause and result, contrast two ideas, or give a different reason.
